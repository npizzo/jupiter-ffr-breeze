Marcus CV1-like anticyclone: four Earth days

The initialized anticyclone remains coherent after four Earth days, while its rim deforms and produces filaments. This is a dry anelastic, prescribed-vortex persistence experiment, without convection or beta. It is a documented reconstruction, not the original authors’ input files.

Top row: vorticity. Bottom row: absolute passive tracer. Three synchronized depths; fixed color mappings throughout, with per-depth vorticity ranges.

What the run shows

At the central saved level, circulation within the fixed initial core ellipse retains 93.0% of its initial value. After subtracting the prescribed background shear contribution, it retains 90.8%. These are fixed-area measurements: deformation and advection can change them. They are not a vortex-energy budget.

Peak recorded Mach is0.132, maximum relative potential-temperature departure is12.0%, and the mass-flux constraint stays below1.3×10⁻¹². The GPU job completed normally in8h27m. Global kinetic energy includes the dominant background flow and should not be used alone to assess vortex persistence.

This demonstrates short-term survival of an initialized anticyclone in this configuration. It does not demonstrate spontaneous formation, preferential sign selection, convergence with resolution, or GRS longevity. The fine-scale rim and surrounding disturbances need to be distinguished from robust vortex dynamics before stronger claims.

Configuration and display

384×384×256, full tilted rotation at23°S, WENO9, no closure, reconstructed stable atmosphere; prescribed shear and initialized CV1-like vortex. Boundary damping and circulation compensation are documented in the source and README.
