Larger initial eddies: 50-day shear comparison

Three completed dry Breeze experiments, each spanning Earth days 0–50. They share the stable upper layer, bottom heating and random initial eddies; only the initial zonal shear differs. The random eddies have wavelength 1,200 km, RMS speed 5.50 m/s and initial Ro = 0.1. Every movie has vorticity above passive tracer, with three synchronized depth columns.

128 × 128 × 64 cells · 6,000 × 6,000 × 120 km · f-plane at 55°N · bottom heating 162 W/m² · no beta · freely evolving initial shear. Sheared cases add peak background speed 27.5 m/s (peak ζ/f = ±0.05); random-flow Ro excludes that background.

Actual heights: 19.69, 59.06 and 100.31 km above the bottom. All 401 three-hourly states are included; playback at 12 frames/s lasts 33.42 seconds. The day-1 restart boundary was checked before joining the segments.

Positive ζ/f is cyclonic here. Vorticity uses a fixed symmetric asinh scale (width 0.1), with per-depth limits shared across all three cases. Tracer is absolute concentration C with a fixed asinh scale (width 0.01, full 0–1 range), shared across times, depths and cases. No framewise rescaling or clipping.

These movies include the evolving zonal flow as well as vortices. An imposed background sign is not evidence of spontaneous vortex selection, and morphology alone does not establish an inverse cascade. This is a coarse screening comparison: 46.875-km grid spacing, twice the original 256² LSV run. The newer sign-symmetric random-mode selection is retained; this is not an exact repeat of that original initial field.
