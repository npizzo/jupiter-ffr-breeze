Does stronger rotation change the vortex sign?

Both cases develop substantial box-scale flow. The slower case has a cyclonic bias. The faster case has weakly negative large-scale filtered skewness but positive full depth-mean skewness: this is not a clean anticyclonic LSV or an established sign transition.

What the saved fields establish

Both began with the same mixed-sign, finite-amplitude large eddies (initial velocity RMS 0.136; wavelength 1.789 in a box of width 4). Rotation-dependent pressure and density balance differ. At 256 × 256 × 80 cells, the model retains the same heating, cap, transport and dissipation as the short tests. No β or imposed shear was added.

The late-window averages cover each case's own final 50 saved time units (187–237 and 213–263); they are not independent ensembles or matched final times. The spectra use density-weighted depth-mean velocities. Large-scale occupancy and skewness do not by themselves demonstrate an inverse energy flux, a single isolated vortex, or a robust preferred sign.

The slower case has positive late-time skewness at all three saved heights. In the faster case the middle and upper slices are weakly negative while the lower slice is positive. Thus the sign depends on depth and filtering; we cannot call the faster run a clean reversal of the whole vortex.

Numerical checks: saved-field spectra reproduce the logged depth-mean kinetic energy to better than 10⁻⁶ relative error; both videos fully decoded on Unity. Mass conservation and finite-state checks passed. These checks do not close the energy budget.
