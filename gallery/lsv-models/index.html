Completed analysis · 19 November 2026 · Unity job 2009617

Plumes spread near the cap; their dynamics are not yet resolved.

This read-only audit examines the three completed higher-resolution movies above, using all 11 daily three-dimensional states and 81 three-hourly depth-mean states in Earth days 40–50. It does not change or restart the simulations. The analysis took 165 seconds on one Unity CPU.

Assessment: rising regions near the stable interface have positive horizontal divergence and negative reconstructed stretching tendency in every case. This is consistent with anticyclonic production by spreading, but the vertical motions are dominated by wavelengths shorter than four grid cells. The result identifies a candidate mechanism to test with a resolved plume; it does not validate a convective closure or establish a shallow anticyclonic LSV.

Energy-transfer estimates and validation limits

The reconstructed horizontal baroclinic-stress work on depth-mean nonzonal modes longer than 2,400 km is −5.86 × 10⁻⁸, −2.68 × 10⁻⁸ and −1.14 × 10⁻⁸ m²/s³ for no shear, cyclonic shear and anticyclonic shear. The corresponding reconstructed barotropic-advection contributions are −7.48 × 10⁻⁷, −3.65 × 10⁻⁸ and −8.60 × 10⁻⁸ m²/s³. These late-window estimates do not show positive feeding by those particular terms, but are not a closed native energy budget and do not describe earlier formation.

The large-scale kinetic energy of the layer-averaged nonzonal velocities is very similar between the lower and upper layers: 4.21 versus 4.23 m²/s² without shear, 1.59 versus 1.59 with cyclonic shear, and 0.343 versus 0.339 with anticyclonic shear. These are bulk band-energy measures, not individual vortex-depth measurements or proof of vertical coherence.

Analytic derivative, spectral projection and Parseval checks pass. The curl reconstructed from independently saved face velocities reproduces the movie vorticity with maximum relative differences below 2.9 × 10⁻⁷. The full-column mass-continuity residual is below 6.1 × 10⁻⁸ relative to the summed layer divergence amplitudes, consistent with Float32 field storage. None of these checks closes the missing timestep-level vorticity budget.

What this changes about the next test

Proceed toward an isolated, spatially resolved plume beneath the existing type of stable interface, with a neutral-layer control and a conditional raised-lid test. Specify horizontal resolution from the prescribed plume width and vertical resolution from the penetration scale; save rapid layerwise tendencies and interface transport. 02 / The polynomial-feedback hypothesis

A small model that can organize large vortices.

This is the original horizontal 2D model with band-limited linear instability and an imposed quadratic/cubic vorticity source. It provides a controlled example of sign-biased vortex organization. Its nonlinear source is a hypothesis about missing convection feedback, not a derived closure of the vertically integrated Breeze equations.

∂tζ + u · ∇ζ = ν∇²ζ + ℱband[ζ] + P0(b f ζ² − c ζ³)

P0 subtracts the spatial mean, preserving zero net vorticity in the periodic box. The quadratic term introduces the sign preference; the cubic term supplies saturation. The linear instability amplifies Fourier modes as ℱ̂band = a|k|²ζ̂ for 12 ≤ |k| ≤ 16, excluding kx = 0.

Here f = ±1 sets the sign of the prescribed feedback. Uniform Coriolis acceleration does not itself supply a vorticity source in this horizontal, incompressible 2D model.

What is feeding the large scales?

The model produces strong sign bias and large coherent vortical structures. To understand that success, we tested whether its quadratic source directly supports the largest modes.

The imposed quadratic term helps maintain the large scales. Positive vorticity skewness persists in both branches even as large-scale energy falls in the intervention. Sign bias and a maintained large-scale vortex are therefore distinct questions.

The earlier formation budget points the same way: over t = 0–400, the quadratic term contributes +0.0783 of energy to these modes, versus +0.0119 from advection. Direct nonlinear source input is substantial; the movie alone should not be described as evidence of an inverse cascade. See the formation audit and maintenance data.

What remains to explain: direct large-scale drive is not inherently unphysical—convective stresses could provide it. But the coefficient, sign dependence and scale dependence of this polynomial have not been derived or validated from convection. One initial state and one resolution do not establish robust formation of a unique LSV.
Model details and interpretation limits

Linear-only control: b = c = 0. Cubic control: b = 0, c = 2 × 10⁻⁴. Biased cases: b = 2 × 10⁻³, c = 2 × 10⁻⁴. Ordinary Laplacian viscosity is present in every case; there is no additional velocity drag or hyperviscosity. The cubic term is the explicitly tested nonlinear saturation.

The maintenance movie has 101 frames at 6 frames/s (16.83 seconds), with a fixed symmetric asinh scale of width 0.1 and limits ±10.55056. Its limits differ from the t = 0–400 movies. Cumulative maintenance budgets use sampled native tendency work, not exact every-stage accounting; the reported final residuals are 2.28 × 10⁻⁷ and 8.64 × 10⁻⁷.

Reading the sign

From a selected sign to a Jovian mechanism.

With right-handed coordinates x east, y north and z upward, ζ = ∂xv − ∂yu. At 55°N, f > 0: positive ζ is cyclonic (counterclockwise when viewed from above). More generally, ζ/f > 0 is cyclonic; ζ/f < 0 is anticyclonic.

The Great Red Spot is anticyclonic. The f-aligned bias prescribed in this polynomial model favors cyclonic vorticity, so its present success at organizing large vortices does not yet explain the Great Red Spot’s sign. The shear experiments test one possible route to changing that outcome; they have not established it.
