Controlled sign-selection hypothesis

Periodic 2π × 2π box; beta=0; ordinary viscosity ν=0.0001. All four cases use the original instability forcing a|k|²ζ in band 12–16, a=0.0002, excluding kₓ=0. Original initial velocities are identical except for the reflected −f case. The new runs use a timestep ceiling 0.0025 with adaptive reductions for stability.

Linear-only reference: b=c=0. Cubic control: b=0, c=0.0002. Biased cases: b=0.002, c=0.0002, f=±1. No selective viscosity or velocity drag is added. The cubic term is the nonlinear vorticity saturation explicitly introduced for this hypothesis.

Positive ζ is counterclockwise and cyclonic for f>0. Negative ζ is cyclonic for f<0. The −f run is a reflected symmetry test, not an independent random realization.

The sign preference is imposed by the quadratic term. These experiments test its effect on large-vortex organization; they do not derive a convection closure or establish a resonance mechanism. Growth of large-scale energy must be compared with direct large-scale energy injection by the polynomial source. Morphology or skewness alone does not demonstrate an inverse cascade.
