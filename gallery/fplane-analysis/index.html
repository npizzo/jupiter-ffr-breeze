Earlier fourteen-case findings

  1. Resolution changes the convective outcome substantially. At 162 W/m², the day-5 RMS depth-mean vorticity is 1.85 × 10⁻⁵ s⁻¹ on 256² and 4.50 × 10⁻⁵ s⁻¹ on 512². Raw skewness rises from 0.029 to 0.199; three-dimensional vorticity skewness changes from −0.026 to +0.475. These runs are not quantitatively converged.
  2. The reconstructed convective source has a cyclonic bias. The conditional mean of V in the most active convection is cyclonic for either sign of f. At day 5, the from-rest raw Z skewness is +0.029 for positive f and −0.052 for negative f. This is consistent with a rotation-linked bias; one realization and five days do not establish its long-time outcome.
  3. Reversing the mature flow does not simply reverse it back within ten days. The reversed run's raw skewness crosses from −0.088 to +0.055, but its central skewness remains negative (−0.339 to −0.206), as does its nonzonal raw skewness (−0.250 to −0.139). A sign change of the raw statistic alone is not proof that the mature anticyclonic structure has disappeared.
  4. The proposed source budget is not closed. The unresolved remainder exceeds the measured tendency in the heated cases. Local source signs are informative, but they cannot yet identify which term quantitatively controls the long-time third moment.

Mature-flow reversal: days 100–110

The upper case continues the heated state unchanged. The lower case reverses only its density-weighted depth-mean horizontal velocity, retaining the baroclinic deviations, vertical velocity, thermodynamics and tracer. This tests response to a reversed large-scale flow, not a reversal of every three-dimensional eddy.

The unchanged case ends with central skewness +0.270; the reversed case ends at −0.206. The interior's mean vorticity and the distribution about that mean matter: raw ⟨Z³⟩/⟨Z²⟩³ᐟ² and central skewness answer different questions. Neither statistic alone measures the survival of a specific tracked vortex.

Resolution changes the answer

Identical physical noise modes seed convection from rest at 162 W/m². The top case uses 256² horizontal cells; the bottom uses 512². Both retain 64 vertical cells and a 15-second timestep ceiling. The fine case produces much stronger vorticity and a clearer cyclonic statistical bias.

This is a sensitivity test, not convergence. The mature-state fine-grid replay is still outstanding in this release. Its initial conservative remap changes kinetic energy by −2.63%; it is a restart experiment, not an independently equilibrated fine-grid condensate.

Heating, initial turbulence and rotation sign

Early turbulence: days 0–5

Both cases start from the same random turbulent velocity field. Heating changes the three-dimensional activity, but the day-5 raw depth-mean skewness remains small: −0.014 with heating and −0.085 with zero bottom heat. This window does not yet explain the eventual single-vortex morphology.

Mature turbulence: days 90–100

These replay the actual late checkpoints of the heated and zero-bottom-heat histories. They share the original initial conditions, but are already different states at day 90. The day-100 central skewness is +0.339 in the heated case and −0.687 in the control. Halving the heated timestep ceiling gives +0.318, a much smaller change than the from-rest resolution difference.

Reverse f, keep its magnitude

In a right-handed system with x east, y north and z up, ζ = ∂v/∂x − ∂u/∂y. Positive ζ is counterclockwise viewed from above. A cyclone has ζ/f > 0: positive vorticity in the positive-f case, negative vorticity in the reversed-f case. The latter is a sign-control experiment, not another northern-hemisphere atmosphere.

Heating is not a monotonic skewness dial

The 5.4 W/m² case has day-5 raw Z skewness 0.675, greater than the 540 W/m² case's 0.081, while its RMS Z is much smaller (6.02 × 10⁻⁷ versus 3.56 × 10⁻⁵ s⁻¹). Normalized skewness alone can make weak, localized onset look more dramatic than vigorous turbulence. This flux sweep probes model sensitivity; it is not a set of calibrated Jovian heat fluxes.

Source audit: useful signs, unresolved balance

The saved three-dimensional velocities give centered reconstructions of V = −2⟨ζ′δ′⟩ and H = −⟨u′·∇ₕζ′⟩ + ⟨ωₕ·∇ₕw⟩. Angle brackets are density-weighted vertical means and primes remove the depth mean. In these completed cases, V is cyclonic on average in the most active convection; H is anticyclonic on average in a 200 km ring outside thresholded cyclonic cores. Those use different conditioning regions and cannot be added as a closed local balance.

At the last mature heated interval, the remainder RMS is 2.27 × 10⁻⁹ s⁻² while the measured storage RMS is 2.60 × 10⁻¹⁰ s⁻². Thus a source-based explanation of the measured evolution is not yet quantitatively verified. Daily or six-hourly three-dimensional fields also undersample fast convection.

The mature heated Z–Ψ relationship is weak in the pooled interior sample: quadratic R² ≈ 0.122 and logistic R² ≈ 0.138. Some logistic fits reach parameter bounds. These fits do not establish equilibrium, a two-level state, or conservation of a third invariant. The effective τ is a descriptive regression coefficient, not a measured dissipation time.

Methods, provenance and next decisions

These are dry anelastic channel experiments with uniform bottom heat flux, a stable upper thermal cap, the existing volume heat-balance correction and an upper vertical-velocity sponge. The cap, correction and sponge remain in zero-bottom-heat controls; “zero bottom heat” therefore does not mean all forcing has been removed. There is no beta effect in this campaign.

The interior excludes the outer 10% at each meridional wall. Full-domain, wall-strip, zonal and nonzonal statistics are retained in the Unity diagnostics. Gaussian smoothing is labeled by FWHM, not a sharp spectral cutoff. Raw model fields remain on Unity; only finished media, diagnostic reports and provenance are published. The downloadable full reports include the reference-density profile and column mass, all spatial masks, filtered distributions, core fractions, fit coefficients, source-scale splits and budget residuals.

  • Finish the mature-state matched-resolution comparison before drawing a grid-robust sign-selection conclusion.
  • Resolve the native discrete vorticity/third-moment budget, or demonstrate temporal and spatial convergence of its reconstruction, before attributing the observed change to V or H.
  • Track coherent structures and repeat selected cases with independent seeds before claiming robust vortex survival or destruction.
  • Longer runs and scale-by-scale energy-transfer measurements are required for an inverse-cascade claim; morphology and skewness alone do not establish it.

The 512²/1024² refinement campaign is held. The current task is to explain vorticity-sign selection in the existing capped, heated f-plane setup: close the full-channel depth-mean vorticity and third-moment budgets, then compare opposite f.
